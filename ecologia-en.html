Ecology of the Canton

Toward a sustainable, inclusive and resilient future

The Ecobarrio Model proposes transforming the human settlements of Poás canton into living communities where environmental sustainability, social equity and urban innovation are integrated as a single fabric. This guide presents its structure, criteria and application process for a truly regenerative territorial development.

▼
Foundation

General Purpose

🎯
Core objective

To contribute to the achievement of the UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and the commitments of the Paris Agreement, promoting human settlements that integrate equity, climate resilience and cultural identity as the axes of urban and territorial design in Poás canton.

🏘️
Approach

The neighborhood as an urban cell is the basic unit of intervention: a space where people coexist, organize and collectively build a safe and sustainable environment. This approach prioritizes the human scale, proximity of services and the active participation of the community in the decisions that shape their territory.

Cross-cutting themes

Cross-Cutting Approaches

♀️
Gender & Intersectionality Approach

Gender mainstreaming is not an add-on, but the axis that articulates the entire model. It recognizes that people experience cities differently based on their gender, and that this difference intersects with other life conditions: disability, migration, rurality and ethnicity. This approach identifies multiple overlapping and mutually reinforcing forms of discrimination.

The specificity of this approach requires designing concrete responses to concrete needs, avoiding universalist solutions that render structural inequalities invisible.

Inclusion Autonomy Empowerment Participation Safety
⚖️
Human Rights

The Ecobarrio is built on inclusion in diversity: the right to the city is universal, and its effective exercise requires removing the physical, social and symbolic barriers that limit access for people in vulnerable situations.

Accessible spaces, designed with a gender perspective, benefit the entire community: people with reduced mobility, caregivers, children and the elderly. Universal accessibility is not a concession, but a condition for shared dignity.

Costa Rica faces a growing housing deficit in a context of accelerated urbanization. The reduction of housing subsidies, pressure on urban land and climate demands make the adoption of innovative models like the Ecobarrio urgent — models that integrate sustainability, equity and inclusion at every stage of housing development.

International references

Lessons from France

The French ÉcoQuartier model offers concrete experiences that illuminate the path for Poás.

💧
Les Arondes

Integration of water resources as the structuring axis of urban design, with full-cycle management and open water spaces accessible to the community.

🏙️
Camille Claudel

Combating urban sprawl through intelligent densification, mixed uses and high-quality public space.

🏭
Novaciéries

Recovery of industrial heritage with mixed uses, new centralities and the memory of place as added value.

🤝
Desjoyaux

Participatory and cooperative housing with high sustainability standards and collective decisions on design.

🌿
Hauts de Feuillis

Passive houses with local ecological materials and bioclimatic design that minimize energy demand from the outset.

🏆
Clichy-Batignolles

Large-scale projects with ÉcoQuartier certification, a European benchmark for ambitious and systemic urban development.

Vision

Conclusion

The Ecobarrio is a community inhabited by diverse people who work in an organized manner, with a sense of belonging, to build a safe, inclusive space in harmony with nature — adapted to the future and regenerative.
